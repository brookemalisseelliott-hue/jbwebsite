Deck vs. patio vs. pergola: which does your Austin backyard need?

A deck is a floor above the ground, a patio is a floor on it, and a pergola is a roof over either. Which one you need comes down to slope, sun, and soil. Compare costs, lifespan, and permit rules for Austin decks, patios, and pergolas.

By John Bernard·Updated September 3, 2026·8 min read

Almost every backyard conversation we have starts the same way: somebody knows they want to fix their outdoor space, but they are not sure what they actually need. Deck? Patio? Pergola? Some combination? The answer usually comes down to three things: how much your yard slopes, how much sun you are fighting, and whether you need a floor or a roof.

Below: what separates a deck, a patio, and a pergola, what each costs to build in Austin, and how to tell which one your yard needs.

Deck vs. patio vs. pergola: what each one is

  • A patio is a floor on the ground. Concrete or pavers, sitting on grade.
  • A deck is a floor above the ground. Framed and elevated, so it can stay level while your yard does not.
  • A pergola or patio cover is a roof. It goes over a patio or deck, and does nothing about the floor.

That last one trips people up most. A pergola is not an alternative to a patio, it is a companion to one.

Choose a patio if your yard is flat

If your back door sits within a step of grade and water already drains away from the house, concrete is the efficient answer. You get the most usable square footage per dollar, nothing to re-stain every couple of years, and a surface that handles a grill, a dining table, and a hundred-degree August without complaint.

Stamped and stained concrete has also come a long way. Done well, it reads far closer to stone than to a driveway. Where concrete struggles is slope: once you need substantial fill or a retaining wall to get a level pad, the cost curve bends sharply upward and a deck starts winning.

Choose a deck if your yard slopes, or you want wood

This is a lot of Austin, especially west and south. Oak Hill, Circle C, Lakeway, Dripping Springs, and much of the Hill Country hand you a backyard that falls away from the house. A framed deck resolves that in a way concrete cannot: the frame absorbs the grade change and gives you a level surface at door height, with stairs down to the yard.

Decks also win when:

  • You want to reach a view instead of sitting below it.
  • You are working around mature oaks and cannot pour a slab over the root zone. A deck can be framed around a tree; concrete cannot.
  • You want the feel of wood underfoot, which is not a small thing on a barefoot summer evening.
  • Your soil is shallow limestone or caliche, where a deck's discrete footings are often less work than excavating a full pad.

The tradeoff is maintenance. Pressure-treated and cedar want re-staining every two to three years in this sun. Composite eliminates that at a higher install cost.

Choose a pergola if the problem is sun, not floor

Here is the test: do you already have a patio you never use? If the surface exists and you still end up inside by 2pm, you do not need more floor. You need shade.

A cedar pergola over an existing slab is the single highest-impact-per-dollar project we build. West-facing backyards in particular go from unusable to comfortable straight through the afternoon. Options range from an open-slat pergola that cuts roughly half the sun, to a polycarbonate or metal-roofed cover that blocks essentially all of it and lets you sit outside during a rainstorm.

While the framing is open is also the cheapest moment to run electrical for ceiling fans, lighting, and speakers. Adding it later means opening things back up.

Combining a deck, patio and pergola in one backyard

The best backyards we build rarely pick just one. A common combination:

  • A stained concrete patio for the grill and dining zone, where you want a hard, heat-tolerant surface.
  • A low cedar deck stepping off the back door, resolving the threshold height and giving a softer seating area.
  • A pergola over the seating half, so that part is usable at 4pm in July.

Doing all three at once costs meaningfully less than adding them one per year, because footings, drainage, and electrical get designed together instead of retrofitted around each other. It is also the version that photographs best if resale is on your mind. See what a deck actually adds to an Austin home's value.

Which one fits your Austin backyard

  • Flat yard, no shade problem → concrete patio.
  • Flat yard, brutal afternoon sun → patio plus pergola.
  • Sloped yard → deck, with stairs to grade.
  • Sloped yard, west-facing → deck plus attached cover.
  • Mature oaks in the way → deck, framed around the trees.
  • Patio you already never use → pergola or patio cover. Skip the new floor.

Whichever direction you land, we handle the HOA architectural review packet and the City of Austin permit as part of the project.

Deck, patio and pergola questions

Is a deck or a concrete patio cheaper in Austin?

For a ground-level space, concrete is usually cheaper: a stamped or stained patio runs roughly $12 to $22 per square foot installed versus $30 to $45 for a pressure-treated deck. That flips as soon as your yard slopes. Once concrete needs significant fill, forming, or a retaining wall to sit level, a framed deck often costs less and looks better.

Do I need a deck if my yard is flat?

Usually not. If your back door sits within a step or two of grade and the yard drains away from the house, a concrete patio gives you more usable surface per dollar and needs almost no maintenance. Decks earn their cost when there is a height difference to resolve, a view to reach, or you specifically want the warmth of wood underfoot.

Does a pergola replace a patio or deck?

No, a pergola is what you put on top of one. It is a shade structure, not a floor. Most of our pergola projects are either added over an existing slab or built as part of a combined deck-and-cover project. If you already have a patio you never sit on because of the sun, a pergola is the highest-impact thing you can add.

Which adds the most resale value in Austin?

A well-built deck and a covered outdoor living space both return well here, because a shaded, usable backyard is a genuine selling point in Central Texas. Bare concrete adds the least emotional appeal even though it is the cheapest to install. If resale is a real factor, the combination that shows best is a patio or deck with a cover over it.

Can you combine a deck, patio, and pergola?

That is the most common project we build. A typical version: a stained concrete patio for the grill and dining area, a low cedar deck stepping off the back door, and a pergola covering the seating half. Designing all three at once is cheaper than adding them one at a time, because the footings, drainage, and electrical get planned together.
