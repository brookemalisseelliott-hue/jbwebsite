Storm damage and hail claims in Austin

Central Texas sits in hail alley, and most springs some part of the metro takes a real storm. Storm work is a large share of what we do, and the insurance side of it is where homeowners most often get taken advantage of. Here is how it actually works.

What hail damage looks like

Most hail damage is not visible from the ground, which is exactly why it goes unclaimed until the roof starts leaking two years later. On asphalt shingles you are looking for soft dark bruises where the granules have been knocked away and the mat underneath is exposed, usually random in pattern rather than in lines. Dented gutters, dinged soft-metal vents, and pockmarks on the AC condenser fins are the giveaways you can check yourself from the ground, because if the hail was hard enough to mark those it was hard enough to mark the roof.

The claim process, step by step

  1. Get an independent inspection first. Before you file, have someone climb it and tell you honestly whether there is a claim worth making. A denied claim still goes on your record.
  2. File with your carrier. Policies carry deadlines for storm claims, and in Texas those windows are shorter than most people assume, so do not sit on it for a season.
  3. The adjuster inspects. We meet them on the roof. This matters more than anything else in the process. An adjuster working alone measures what they see; an adjuster walking it with your contractor sees the vents, flashing, and decking that belong in the scope.
  4. Review the scope, not just the total. Insurers write a line-itemed scope. If it leaves out drip edge, ridge vent, or the second layer of underlayment your roof actually has, that gets supplemented, and supplements are routine, not confrontational.
  5. Understand what you are being paid. Most policies pay actual cash value first, then release the recoverable depreciation once the work is done and invoiced. You pay your deductible and nothing beyond it.
  6. We invoice the carrier directly and hand you the final paperwork and the warranty.

Things to be careful about after a storm

Austin fills up with out-of-state crews within days of a big hail event. Some are fine. Many will be unreachable by the time your roof leaks.

  • Never sign an assignment of benefits. If a contract hands your contractor the right to deal with your insurer on your behalf, you have signed away control of your own claim. Read what you sign.
  • Nobody should offer to cover your deductible. It is insurance fraud in Texas, and the homeowner is exposed too, not only the contractor.
  • Do not let a door-knocker climb your roof before you have agreed to anything. Damage caused during an uninvited "inspection" is a real problem with a company that no longer answers the phone.
  • Check they are local and will still exist in five years. A manufacturer warranty means nothing if nobody is here to service the workmanship side of it.
  • Be suspicious of pressure. Hail damage does not expire in 48 hours. Anyone telling you it does wants a signature before you get a second opinion.

Choosing a roof for Austin heat

Class 4 impact-rated shingles

These are tested against steel balls dropped to simulate hail, and in hail country they are usually worth the upgrade twice over: once because the roof survives a storm that would total a standard shingle, and once because most Texas carriers discount your premium for having them. Ask your agent what the discount is before you decide; it often pays back a good share of the difference.

Standing-seam metal

Forty to sixty years, reflects a great deal of the solar load instead of holding it, and sheds hail rather than bruising. It costs roughly double a shingle roof and it is the last roof many people buy. On Hill Country homes it also simply looks right.

Ventilation, which is the part nobody quotes

An Austin attic in August can run well over 140 degrees, and a roof that cannot breathe cooks its own shingles from underneath and shortens its life by years. Balanced intake at the soffit and exhaust at the ridge is not an upgrade, it is what makes the shingle warranty mean anything. If a quote says nothing about ventilation, ask why.

Roofing questions from Austin homeowners

How much does a new roof cost in Austin?

Architectural asphalt shingles, the most common choice on Austin homes, run $5.50 to $9.00 per square foot installed, which lands most 2,000-square-foot homes between $11,000 and $18,000. Standing seam metal runs $12.50 to $22.00 per square foot, or $25,000 to $45,000 on the same house. Pitch, tear-off complexity, decking condition, and underlayment all move the number, so we inspect before quoting.

Do you handle insurance claims for storm damage roofs?

Yes, and it is a large share of our work. We inspect free, document the damage with photos, meet your adjuster on the roof so the scope reflects what is actually up there, handle supplements when the first scope misses something, and invoice the carrier directly. You pay your deductible and nothing beyond it. We will not pressure you into filing a claim you don't need, and we will tell you when there isn't one.

How do I know if my Austin roof has hail damage?

Most hail damage is invisible from the ground. From the yard, check your gutters, soft-metal roof vents, and the fins on your AC condenser: if hail was hard enough to dent those, it was hard enough to bruise your shingles. On the roof itself, the sign is soft dark spots where granules have been knocked off, scattered randomly rather than in lines. We inspect for free and tell you honestly either way.

How long do I have to file a hail damage claim in Texas?

Your policy sets the deadline and those windows are shorter than most homeowners expect, so do not let a season pass. Check your policy language or ask your agent directly. The practical advice is simple: if a significant hail storm hit your neighborhood, get the roof looked at within weeks rather than remembering it next spring when it starts leaking.

Should I use a roofer who knocks on my door after a storm?

Be careful. Austin fills with out-of-state crews after every major hail event, and many are gone before your workmanship warranty ever matters. Never sign an assignment of benefits, which hands over control of your own claim. Nobody should offer to cover your deductible, which is fraud in Texas and exposes you as well as them. And do not let anyone on your roof before you have agreed to anything. Real hail damage does not expire in 48 hours, whatever you are told.

How long does a roof replacement take?

Most asphalt shingle roof replacements on a typical Austin home are completed in 1 to 2 days. Standing seam metal takes 3 to 5 days, and larger or multi-plane roofs can run 4 to 7. We set the schedule with you in advance and text daily progress updates.

Do I need to replace my whole roof or can I repair it?

It depends on age, scope of damage, and the underlayment condition. Roofs under 10 years old with isolated damage can usually be repaired. Roofs 15+ years old, with widespread hail bruising, missing shingles, or visible underlayment damage typically need full replacement. We give you an honest read after a free inspection. We never up-sell what isn't there.

Are Class 4 impact-rated shingles worth it in Austin?

In hail country, usually yes. They are tested against simulated hail and survive storms that would total a standard shingle, and most Texas insurers discount your premium for having them. Ask your agent what the discount is on your policy before deciding, because it frequently pays back a good portion of the upgrade over the life of the roof.

What roofing materials work best in the Texas Hill Country?

Architectural asphalt shingles in a Class 4 impact rating are the most common and offer good hail resistance for the money. Standing-seam metal lasts 40 to 60 years, reflects heat, and qualifies for discounts under many policies. Concrete tile and synthetic slate suit higher-end Hill Country builds. We help you pick based on look, budget, and how long you plan to stay in the home.
