Patio covers in Cedar Park

This is the most requested project we get in Cedar Park, and it makes sense. A west-facing Cedar Park patio is unusable from May to September without a roof over it. A solid cover turns that concrete into a room you actually use five extra months a year.

There are two ways to build one.

An attached patio cover ties into your home's framing or roofline. It looks like it came with the house, it sheds water away from the foundation when it is flashed properly, and it almost always needs a permit and often an engineer's stamp. Attached covers typically run $1,500 to $4,000 more than the equivalent freestanding structure because of the tie-in and flashing work.

A freestanding pavilion stands on its own posts out in the yard, usually over a patio, a pool deck, or an outdoor kitchen. No roofline tie-in, no flashing detail, and you can put it wherever the shade is actually needed rather than wherever the house allows.

For roofing on top, most Cedar Park covers get shingles matched to the house, standing-seam metal, or polycarbonate panels that pass light through while stopping rain. Metal costs more up front and reflects a lot of heat, which matters on a Hill Country lot with no mature canopy.

The upgrade that changes how a cover feels more than anything else is a tongue-and-groove ceiling. Bare rafters read as utilitarian. A finished pine or cedar T&G ceiling reads as a room. It runs $12 to $25 per square foot installed, so about $2,700 to $5,600 on a 14x16.

Pergolas in Cedar Park

A pergola is the open-top version: posts, beams, and slats overhead. It defines a space and throws dappled shade rather than blocking sun outright. In Cedar Park we build a lot of them over fire pit areas and off the back of homes where an attached cover would block too much light from interior windows.

Cedar is the standard here. We use #2 Common kiln-dried Western Red Cedar on most builds. Clear-grade cedar has no knots and costs roughly double, and once a pergola is stained almost nobody can tell the difference from ten feet away.

The part that matters and that you will never see is the footings. Cedar Park sits on caliche, and posts need to go 24 to 36 inches down, often into rock. That is auger work and concrete at $150 to $400 per post. A six-post structure has $900 to $2,400 of footing in it before a single beam goes up. It is also the first thing a cheap quote skips.

Screened porches and sunrooms

This is the fastest-growing request we get out here, and it is worth explaining the difference because the terms get used interchangeably.

A screened porch is a covered structure with screen panels instead of walls. You get the shade and the rain protection of a patio cover plus the thing Cedar Park homeowners actually want, which is sitting outside at dusk in June without being eaten alive. It is not conditioned space, it does not need insulation or HVAC, and it costs far less than a true sunroom.

A sunroom is glazed, insulated, and usually conditioned. It is an addition, with everything that implies for permitting, foundation, and price.

For most Cedar Park backyards, the screened porch is the better value by a wide margin. A finished T&G ceiling, a stained pine floor, and framed screen openings gets you a genuine three-season room for a fraction of what an addition costs.

Not sure which one you want? That is a normal place to start. John will walk the space, tell you what the lot and the roofline will actually allow, and price the options side by side. No charge and no pressure.

Permits and HOA approval in Cedar Park

Cedar Park spans Williamson and Travis counties, and most of what we build inside city limits needs a permit. Attached patio covers, structures that tie into the house, decks above a certain height, and anything electrical generally do. Freestanding structures sometimes do and sometimes do not depending on size and placement. We pull permits as part of the job rather than leaving it on you, and we will tell you honestly which category your project falls into before you sign anything.

Separately, most Cedar Park subdivisions have an active HOA with architectural review. Buttercup Creek, Twin Creeks, Reagan's Overlook and Block House Creek all do. That review can take a few weeks, so it is worth starting early. We prepare and submit the architectural packet, including drawings and material specs, as part of every project.

Cedar Park questions, answered

How much does a patio cover cost in Cedar Park?

An attached open-top patio cover typically runs $10,500 to $16,000 installed. A solid-roof cover in cedar with shingle or metal runs $14,500 to $22,000, and larger or heavily upgraded structures run $22,500 to $38,000. Size, roofing material, and whether you add a tongue-and-groove ceiling are the three biggest variables.

Do I need a permit for a patio cover in Cedar Park?

Usually yes for an attached cover, since it ties into the home's structure, and often an engineer's stamp as well. Freestanding structures depend on size and placement. We pull permits as part of the project and will tell you which category yours falls into before you commit to anything.

What is the difference between a pergola and a patio cover?

A pergola has an open top made of beams and slats, so it throws dappled shade and still lets light and some rain through. A patio cover has a solid roof, so it blocks sun and rain entirely. In Cedar Park's summer heat a solid cover is genuinely useful five or more months a year, while a pergola is more about defining the space.

Should I build a screened porch or a sunroom?

For most Cedar Park homes, a screened porch is the better value. It gives you shade, rain protection, and bug-free evenings without being conditioned space. A sunroom is glazed, insulated and usually climate-controlled, which makes it an addition with addition-level permitting and cost.

Can you build decks on sloped Cedar Park lots?

Yes, and it is most of what we do here. We build elevated and multi-level decks with engineered footings sized for the actual grade. On a real slope, stepping the deck down in levels usually beats one tall platform on both cost and how it feels to use.

Do you handle Cedar Park HOA approvals?

Yes. Most Cedar Park subdivisions have active architectural review, including Buttercup Creek, Twin Creeks, Reagan's Overlook and Block House Creek. We prepare and submit the packet for fences, decks, pergolas, covers and exterior changes as part of every project. Allow a few weeks for review.

Do you serve all of Cedar Park?

Yes, every Cedar Park neighborhood, plus Leander, Jollyville, Brushy Creek, Round Rock and the rest of northwest Travis and Williamson counties.
