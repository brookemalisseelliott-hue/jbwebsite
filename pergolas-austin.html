Pergola or patio cover?

The short version: if the problem is heat, an open cedar pergola cuts the worst of the afternoon sun, keeps the yard feeling open, and is the cheapest of the options. If the problem is rain, a pergola sheds almost nothing and you want a solid roof instead. We cover that build in full on our Austin patio covers page, including pricing, roof types and the tie-in detail.

The thing nobody mentions: which way it faces

Shade is a function of rafter direction, spacing, and depth, not just of having a structure overhead. A pergola whose rafters run the wrong way for your sun angle will look right and shade almost nothing at 5pm, which is exactly when you want to sit outside. We work out the orientation against where your sun actually comes from before we settle the design, and on a west-facing patio we will usually recommend deeper rafters, tighter spacing, or simply a solid roof.

Materials, and what holds up here

  • Western Red Cedar. Our default and the right answer for most Austin backyards. Naturally rot and insect resistant, takes stain well, and holds its shape through our wet-dry swings better than pine.
  • Pressure-treated pine. Cheaper up front and structurally fine. It moves more and wants re-staining more often. Worth asking any contractor directly which one they are quoting, because pine stained cedar-colour is a common substitution.
  • Steel and aluminium. Thinner posts and longer clear spans, and no staining ever. Reads more modern, and it gets hot.
  • Polycarbonate panels. Blocks the rain while keeping the room behind the patio bright. A good compromise when a solid roof would make the kitchen dark.

Austin-specific things worth knowing

Attaching to the house is the part that goes wrong

Most failures we get called to repair are not the posts, they are the ledger where the structure meets the house. Flashed incorrectly, it puts water straight into your wall sheathing and you will not notice until the damage is expensive. If a quote does not mention flashing at all, ask about it.

Wind

A solid roof is a sail. A cover that would be perfectly adequate in a calmer climate needs proper post anchoring and uplift connections here, especially on an exposed lot west of town. This is engineering, not preference.

Permits and HOAs

Smaller freestanding pergolas often do not need a permit, while attached covers and anything with electrical typically do, and each city around Austin draws that line differently. Separately, most subdivisions want architectural review before anything goes up in the backyard, and several have specific rules about roof pitch and materials matching the house. We prepare the HOA packet and pull the permits as part of the project.

Footings in our soil

Central Austin clay swells and shrinks through the year. Posts set too shallow will move, and on a rigid roofed structure that movement shows up as racking and cracked joints. Depth is not the place to save money.

Pergola questions from Austin homeowners

How much does a pergola cost in Austin?

A freestanding 12x12 cedar pergola in Austin typically runs $8,500 to $13,000 installed. An attached open-top patio cover of the same size runs about $10,500 to $16,000, and a solid-roof cover starts around $14,500. Larger structures, tongue-and-groove ceilings, lighting, and fans push the number up from there.

Pergola or patio cover, what's the difference?

A pergola has open beams overhead, providing partial shade and air flow. A patio cover has a solid roof (shingle, metal, or polycarbonate) that fully blocks sun and rain. Many Austin homeowners pick a patio cover for full Texas-summer protection, or a pergola for a softer, more open look. If keeping dry matters at all, choose the solid roof.

How long does a pergola install take?

Most freestanding cedar pergolas are completed in 4 to 7 days. Attached patio covers with electrical for lighting, fans, or speakers usually run 1 to 2 weeks. HOA architectural review is often the longest part of the timeline, so it is worth starting that early.

Do pergolas need permits in Austin?

Smaller freestanding pergolas often do not require a building permit, while attached patio covers and any structure with electrical typically do, and every city around Austin sets that threshold slightly differently. We pull every permit needed as part of the project.

How much shade does a pergola actually give?

It depends almost entirely on rafter depth, spacing, and which direction they run relative to your sun. A well-designed cedar pergola over a west-facing patio can take the edge off the worst of the late afternoon. A poorly oriented one will look good and shade almost nothing when you need it most. We work the orientation out against your actual sun angle during the design, and if the numbers do not get there we will tell you to consider a solid roof instead.

Can you add a pergola over an existing concrete patio?

Usually yes, and it is one of the most common things we do. The question is whether the existing slab is thick enough and sound enough to carry post loads. Sometimes we can anchor to it directly, and sometimes we core through and set footings below. We check this at the estimate rather than discovering it on build day.

Can you wire in fans, lights, and outlets?

Yes, and we run it inside the framing during the build so nothing is surface-mounted afterwards. Ceiling fans make a real difference under a solid cover in an Austin August, and they need proper structural blocking, which is far easier to do while the structure is going up than to retrofit later.

Will a pergola add value to my Austin home?

Covered outdoor living is one of the more reliably valued upgrades in this market, because the season for using a backyard here is long and the barrier to using it is shade. A well-built, permitted structure that matches the house reads as finished square footage to a buyer. A wobbly unpermitted one reads as a repair item. We looked at the resale numbers here.
