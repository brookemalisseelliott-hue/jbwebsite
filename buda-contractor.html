Custom deck installation in Buda

This is the work we do most in Buda and the work we are best known for here. Most Buda lots are flatter than what we deal with out toward the Hill Country, which changes the build. On flat ground you are usually choosing between a low ground-level deck that sits just above grade and a raised deck that ties into a back door threshold.

Ground-level decks are the better value on a flat Buda lot. No guardrail required under 30 inches, shorter posts, less lumber, and nothing blocking the view of the yard. You still need proper footings and a real ledger connection, and you still need airflow underneath or the framing will rot from the bottom up.

Raised decks make sense when the back door sits well above grade, which is common in the newer Sunfield sections. Once you are over 30 inches you need code-compliant guardrail, and stairs become a real part of the budget rather than an afterthought.

On material, pressure-treated pine is what most Buda decks get and it is the right call for most budgets. Cedar costs more, smells better, and weathers to a nicer grey. Composite costs the most up front and never needs staining, though it runs hot underfoot in an unshaded Buda backyard in July, and a lot of these lots have no mature canopy yet.

A typical pressure-treated deck in Buda runs $10,500 to $26,000 installed depending on size, height, railing, and whether there is a cover over it.

What deck installation in Buda actually involves

  1. Free on-site walk. John measures the space, checks the door threshold height, looks at where water runs after a storm, and tells you what the lot will actually carry.
  2. Line-itemed quote. Framing, decking, railing, stairs, hardware and finish separated out, so you can see what a railing upgrade or a bigger footprint actually costs you.
  3. HOA packet and permit. Garlic Creek, Sunfield, Whispering Hollow and Elm Grove all run architectural review. We prepare and submit it. Hays County or City of Buda permitting runs alongside, depending on which side of the line you are on.
  4. Footings and framing. Piers dug to depth, posts set, beams and joists sized for the span. This is the part nobody ever sees and the only part that determines whether the deck is still solid in fifteen years.
  5. Decking, railing and stairs. Hidden fasteners on composite, face-screwed and set flush on wood. Stair rise kept consistent, because uneven stairs are the thing people notice with their feet every single day.
  6. Seal, clean, walk it. Wood gets sealed once it has dried down to the right moisture content, not the day it goes down. We magnet-sweep the yard and walk the finished deck with you.

Most Buda deck installations are a one to two week build. The paperwork ahead of it is usually the longer half, so the single best thing you can do to pull your finish date forward is let us start the HOA submission the day you sign.

Covered patios and patio covers in Buda

A deck in Buda without shade over it gets used in April and October and sits empty the rest of the year. That is the honest truth about west-facing backyards out here with no trees yet.

An attached patio cover ties into the house roofline, needs a permit and usually an engineer's stamp, and runs $10,500 to $16,000 for an open top or $14,500 to $22,000 with a solid roof. A freestanding pergola stands on its own posts and starts around $8,500.

If you are building a deck now and thinking about a cover later, tell us before we frame it. Sizing the posts and footings for a future roof at build time costs very little. Retrofitting a cover onto a deck that was not designed to carry one means tearing into finished work.

Concrete patios and hardscape in Buda

Not every backyard wants a deck. On a flat Buda lot a poured or stamped concrete patio is often the better answer: it costs less per square foot, it never needs staining, and it gives a pergola or cover a solid base to sit on.

We pour stamped and broom-finish patios, paver walkways, and seat walls. Buda sits on blackland clay in places, which moves more with moisture than the caliche north of the river does, so slab thickness and reinforcement matter more here than people expect. We size it for the soil rather than pouring everything four inches and hoping.

If a cover is in your future, setting footings at pour time costs $400 to $1,200 and saves cutting into finished concrete later.

Hardie siding in Buda

The original siding on a lot of Buda's 2010-era housing stock is reaching the age where it starts failing at the bottom course, usually where sprinklers have been hitting it for a decade. Once water gets behind it, it wicks up into the sheathing and you stop having a siding problem and start having a framing problem.

We replace with fiber cement, normally James Hardie. It runs $10 to $16 per square foot installed, with a full re-side on a typical Buda home landing between $22,000 and $58,000 depending on size and profile.

We tear the old siding off rather than going over it. Going over it hides exactly the rot you are paying us to find, and we would rather show you a photo of bad sheathing than cover it up.

Cedar privacy fences in Buda

Six-foot cedar privacy fence in board-on-board, shadowbox, or horizontal slat. Cedar runs $32 to $55 per linear foot installed depending on style, post spacing, and whether there is old fence to tear out.

Most Buda subdivisions have fence rules in the HOA covenants covering height, style, and which side the finished face has to point. We check those before quoting so you are not rebuilding a fence you just paid for.

Roof replacement and storm damage in Buda

Buda sits in the hail corridor and gets hit most springs. We do free inspections after a storm, and we will meet your insurance adjuster at the house so somebody is standing there who can point at the damage while they are on the roof.

If your roof does not need replacing, we will tell you. We would rather lose the job than sell you a roof you do not need.
