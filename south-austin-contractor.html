The two things that make a South Austin build different

This is the part a contractor from up north tends to find out the hard way, on your dime.

  • Rock. Much of 78748, Oak Hill, and Circle C sits on shallow limestone and caliche. Post holes frequently need coring instead of augering, and footings get set to the rock line rather than a default depth. We price that up front. If someone quotes your Circle C deck without asking about rock, they are planning to discover it as a change order.
  • Mature trees and root systems. South Austin lots come with old live oaks, and the good ones are worth designing around. That means hand-digging near critical root zones, adjusting beam layout instead of cutting roots, and sometimes floating a deck section rather than sinking a post. Trees are the reason your backyard is worth improving. We do not trade one for the other.

How much does a deck or fence cost in South Austin?

Cedar privacy fence runs about $35 to $55 per linear foot installed. Decks run $30 to $45 per square foot in pressure-treated pine, $50 to $75 in cedar, and $60 to $100 in composite. A cedar pergola typically lands between $6,000 and $18,000 depending on span and whether it is attached or freestanding. Do you handle South Austin HOA approvals?

Yes. Circle C, Shady Hollow, Bauerle Ranch, Western Oaks, and Onion Creek all run active architectural review committees with their own packet requirements. Does South Austin's limestone and caliche soil affect deck footings?

It does, and it is the main thing that separates a South Austin build from a build up north. Much of 78748, Oak Hill, and Circle C sits on shallow limestone and caliche. Post holes often need coring rather than augering, and footing depth gets engineered to the rock line rather than a default number. We price that in up front instead of discovering it as a change order on day two.
