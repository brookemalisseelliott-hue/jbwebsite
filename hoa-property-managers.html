For your homeowners: ARC submissions that arrive complete

Most of our work is for individual homeowners, and most of them live in an HOA. Every one of those projects goes through architectural review, and we prepare the packet ourselves:

  1. We read the CCRs first and design to them, so the board is not asked to approve a 7 foot fence in a 6 foot neighborhood.
  2. We submit a complete packet: your form, a site plan with setbacks, an elevation or photo example, and the exact material and stain color.
  3. We do not start until the approval letter is in hand.
  4. We build what was approved. If something has to change on site, we come back to the ARC before we change it.

If you keep a list of contractors for homeowners who ask, we would be glad to be on it. Our guide to HOA approval in Austin is written for homeowners and is free to share in a newsletter or on your community portal.

Twenty minutes at your board or ARC meeting.

Questions community managers ask us

Can you send a W-9 and certificate of insurance?

Yes. Tell us the association's legal name and we will have our insurance agent issue a certificate naming the association, and your management company if you need it, as additional insured. The W-9 comes the same day.

Will you register on our vendor compliance portal?

Yes. Whatever your company uses for vendor onboarding, send the invite and we will complete it, including the annual renewal.

Do you do small common-area repairs, or only big projects?

Both. A run of leaning perimeter fence or a few rotted pickets at the amenity center is exactly the kind of job boards struggle to get anyone to show up for. We will quote it the same way we quote a full replacement.

How do you handle homeowners in our community who hire you?

We read the CCRs before we design anything, prepare a complete architectural review packet with a site plan, materials and colors, and submit it with the homeowner's signature. We do not start until the approval letter is in hand, and we build what was approved.

Can John walk the property before the board meets?

Yes. A walkthrough and a written, line-itemed estimate are free, so the board has a real number for the budget or reserve discussion rather than a guess.
