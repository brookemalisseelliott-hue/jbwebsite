Two kinds of ground in one city

Leander sits right where the Hill Country starts, and the ground changes depending on which side of town you are on.

West Leander, out toward Crystal Falls and Travisso, is sloped, with limestone often close to the surface. Footings get drilled into rock rather than dug, and a backyard that falls away from the house usually means an elevated deck. Once a deck is more than 30 inches off the ground it needs code guardrail, which is worth knowing before you fall in love with a design.

East Leander is flatter, with more clay in the soil. Clay swells when it is wet and shrinks in a drought, so posts need to go deep enough to sit below that movement. It is the most common reason a fence starts leaning or a gate stops latching after a couple of years.

Either way, we look at the lot before we price it, so the footing work is in the estimate rather than a surprise on day one.

New-build backyards: shade first

On a lot with no mature trees, a deck or patio in full afternoon sun gets very little use from June to September. On a lot like that, a patio cover or a pergola is usually the better first project, and a deck or extended patio can follow. If you do want composite decking, go with a lighter color, because dark boards get too hot to walk on barefoot in summer.

Builder fences are hitting replacement age

A lot of Leander homes came with a basic pine fence. Many of those are now old enough that the posts are rotting at the ground line and whole sections are starting to lean. If it is only a few posts, a repair can buy years. If most of the line is going, replacing it with cedar on deeper posts costs less over the life of the fence than repairing it section by section.

Permits and HOA review in Leander

The City of Leander permits structures inside the city limits, and homes outside the city fall under Williamson or Travis County. We check which rules apply to your address before we quote.
