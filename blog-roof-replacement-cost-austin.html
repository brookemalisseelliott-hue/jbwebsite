Roof replacement cost in Austin

Real 2026 numbers on new roofs in Austin. Architectural shingle, standing seam metal, and synthetic slate ranges, plus what insurance actually covers after a Texas hailstorm and how to spot a quote that's too good to be true.

By John Bernard·Updated June 12, 2026·9 min read

A roof replacement is one of the biggest single-line-item home repairs you'll ever pay for, and in Austin it's also one of the most common. Hail in spring, sustained UV exposure through summer, the occasional ice event in winter. Most Austin roofs don't make it to their factory-warranty lifespan. They get cut short by a storm.

So here's what a new roof actually costs in Austin in 2026, what changes the number, what your insurance will (and won't) cover, and what to look for in a quote so you don't end up with a $14,000 roof that leaks in three years.

Most roof replacements in greater Austin fall into one of these ranges. These are total installed prices on a typical single-story 2,000-square-foot home (around 22 to 25 "squares" of roof). Material, labor, tear-off, underlayment, flashing, ridge vent, and cleanup are all included:

What drives roof replacement cost up or down in Austin

1. Material is the biggest single factor

The gap between basic 3-tab shingles and standing seam metal can be $30,000 on the same house. Material choice is where the bulk of the price decision happens, and it's also the most important long-term decision: a $14,000 architectural shingle roof and a $32,000 metal roof both keep water out today, but one will need replacing in 20 years and the other in 50.

2. Tear-off and what we find underneath

A roof replacement isn't just new shingles on top of old. We strip everything down to the decking, inspect for rot, replace bad sheathing, and put new underlayment down. If your home has had two roofs already (you can usually see this from the rake edge), code requires full tear-off. That adds $1,500 to $4,000 to most jobs. If we find rotted sheathing under the old roof (especially common on homes with skipped roof maintenance or prior leaks), each sheet of plywood we replace runs about $80 to $150 installed.

3. Pitch and complexity

A simple gable roof on a single-story house is the cheapest to install. Hips, valleys, dormers, skylights, multiple chimneys, and steep pitches all add real time. A steep 12-pitch roof can take 50 percent longer than a 6-pitch and requires safety harnessing, which shows up in labor. A roof with 6 dormers and a chimney has $3,000+ of flashing work that a plain gable doesn't.

4. Underlayment choice

This is the layer between your roof deck and the shingles. The cheap option is 30-pound felt paper (still code-legal, not what we'd put on our own home). The standard now is synthetic underlayment, which doesn't tear in wind, holds nails better, and costs about $400 to $900 more per house. On lower-slope sections, valleys, and roof-wall intersections, we always use ice-and-water shield, which is a self-sealing peel-and-stick membrane. Saving $600 on underlayment to save $80,000 in interior water damage is not a trade we make.

5. Ridge vent, soffit vent, and ventilation

A properly ventilated attic extends roof life by 25 to 40 percent and cuts your summer cooling bill noticeably. A ridge vent installation runs $500 to $1,500 depending on linear footage. Soffit vent baffles to balance the airflow add another $300 to $800. If your roof is going on a poorly ventilated attic, you'll see shingles aging faster than they should. We never skip the vent calc.

6. Flashing, drip edge, and the boring details

The places where the roof meets a wall, a chimney, a skylight, or a vent pipe are where roofs leak first. New flashing, new drip edge along all eaves and rakes, and new pipe boots are all standard on a real replacement. Some contractors will reuse old flashing to save $400 to $1,200. Don't let them. Flashing is the cheapest part of a roof and the part that fails first if it's wrong.

Shingle vs. metal vs. synthetic roof cost compared

Architectural (dimensional) asphalt shingles

The most-installed roof in Austin and the most cost-effective for most homes. Modern architectural shingles have 30-year warranties, hold up well to hail (especially Class 4 impact-rated versions, which can earn you 10 to 25 percent off your homeowner's insurance), and look great. In Austin's UV and storms, realistic lifespan is 18 to 25 years. Available in dozens of colors. The sweet spot for 80 percent of Austin homes.

3-tab asphalt shingles

The budget option. Flat, thin, and going away. Many newer neighborhoods don't allow 3-tab anymore via HOA rules, and insurance carriers are increasingly reluctant to insure them on Texas homes. Lifespan in Austin's climate is realistically 12 to 18 years. We don't recommend them unless you're flipping or selling soon.

Luxury / designer shingles

Thicker, heavier shingles that mimic slate, wood shake, or premium architectural patterns. Brands like CertainTeed Presidential, GAF Grand Sequoia, Owens Corning Berkshire. Cost 50 to 80 percent more than standard architectural but last 30 to 40 years and have curb appeal that's hard to match without going to real slate. Worth it on higher-end homes or for sellers who want a roof that adds value.

Standing seam metal

The premium option for Texas. Reflects heat (a real factor in Austin's 100-degree summers, often cuts AC bills 10 to 25 percent), lasts 40 to 60 years, sheds hail damage where shingles would dent. Modern standing seam panels come in dozens of colors and don't have the "barn roof" look of older metal. The cost is real (2 to 3x shingle) but the per-year cost over 50 years often beats shingles. Great fit on modern, farmhouse, and Hill Country architecture. We install a lot of these.

Synthetic slate and shake

Polymer or composite tiles that look like real slate or cedar shake. Lighter than the real thing (so they don't need structural reinforcement), Class 4 impact-rated, and warrantied 30 to 50 years. Brands like DaVinci, EcoStar, F-Wave. Premium look without the weight or the cost of real slate (which runs $40+ per square foot installed). Great on architecture that calls for that look.

How Austin roof insurance claims work

Most Austin roofs get replaced through an insurance claim, not out of pocket. After a major hail or wind event, here's the realistic process:

  1. Document the damage. Take photos from the ground (especially dented gutters, dented A/C fins, splatter marks on fences and decks). These are corroborating evidence the adjuster will look for.
  2. Get an inspection from a roofer before calling insurance. A good roofer will tell you honestly whether there's enough damage to file a claim. Filing a claim that gets denied is worse than not filing.
  3. Call your insurance company and open a claim. They'll send an adjuster within 5 to 14 days in most cases. After major hail storms, that can stretch to 30+ days.
  4. Have your roofer meet the adjuster on-site. This is the single most important step. Adjusters often miss damage, especially on backslopes or hip ridges. A roofer who knows what to point out gets the claim approved more often than not.
  5. Get the adjuster's report and scope of loss. This is the dollar amount your insurance approved. It should include shingles, underlayment, drip edge, flashing, ridge vent, and reasonable labor. If they're missing things, your roofer can supplement.
  6. Pick your contractor and authorize the work. Your insurance check goes to you (and your mortgage company if applicable). You then pay your contractor.

You'll typically pay your deductible (often $1,000 to $5,000 depending on your policy), and insurance covers the rest. Any contractor who offers to "eat the deductible" is committing insurance fraud and will get both of you in trouble. Don't work with them.

How long does a roof replacement take?

Most shingle roof replacements on single-story Austin homes take 1 to 2 working days from tear-off to cleanup. Two-story homes with multiple roof planes can take 3 to 4 days. Standing seam metal takes 3 to 5 days because the panels are custom-cut and require more careful installation. Permits in Austin add 1 to 3 weeks before we can start. We pull the permit, schedule the inspection, and handle all the back-and-forth with the city.

Where to save on roof replacement cost (and where not to)

  • Time the project around a real damage event. If you have hail damage, file the claim. A $14,000 roof you pay $2,500 for after a covered claim is the best deal in home repair.
  • Pick Class 4 impact-rated shingles. Costs 5 to 15 percent more upfront, can earn you 10 to 25 percent off your homeowners' insurance for the life of the roof. Often pays for itself in 4 to 6 years.
  • Don't reuse old flashing. This is the cheapest place a bad contractor will try to "save you money." Don't. Flashing is the part that leaks first if it's wrong.
  • Verify the roofer's manufacturer certification. Most shingle warranties are only fully transferable if the roofer is certified by the manufacturer. We are GAF Certified and Owens Corning Preferred. That matters when you sell the house.
  • Don't skip the underlayment upgrade. Saving $600 on felt vs. synthetic to maybe pay $80,000 in interior water damage is a terrible trade.
  • Get the ventilation right. A poorly ventilated roof ages 25 to 40 percent faster. Spend $800 on ridge and soffit vents to add 4 to 8 years to the roof.

How to spot a quote that's too good to be true

If a contractor quotes you 30 percent below the ranges in the table above, ask hard questions. The usual ways a too-cheap quote gets to a too-cheap number:

  • Reusing old flashing, drip edge, and pipe boots. Sometimes hidden in the line item as "labor only."
  • Felt paper instead of synthetic underlayment. Saves $400 to $900. Real risk in storms.
  • Skipping the ridge or soffit vent. Saves $500 to $1,500. Will cut roof life by years.
  • 3-tab shingles passed off as "architectural." Watch the brand and model number, not the salesperson's wording.
  • No warranty registration with the manufacturer. Means your 30-year warranty isn't real.
  • "Eating the deductible." Illegal. Don't do it.

What to budget for a roof replacement in Austin

A real architectural shingle roof in Austin in 2026 will run you somewhere between $11,000 and $18,000 for a typical single-story home. A metal roof will run $25,000 to $45,000 and last twice as long. The biggest cost drivers are material choice, complexity, and whether tear-off finds any rotted decking. The biggest quality drivers are the underlayment, the flashing, the ventilation, and the manufacturer certification of your roofer.

If you're trying to figure out whether a quote you got is honest, ask specifically: what underlayment brand, what flashing material, what warranty, what ridge vent, what manufacturer certification. A real roofer will answer in specific brand names and numbers. A cheap quote will get vague.

Roof replacement questions from Austin homeowners

How much does a roof replacement cost in Austin?

A standard architectural shingle roof replacement on a 2,000-square-foot Austin home typically runs $11,000 to $18,000 installed. Standing seam metal runs $25,000 to $45,000. Synthetic slate and luxury shingles fall in between.

Does insurance cover a roof replacement in Austin?

Yes, if the damage is from a covered event like hail, wind, or fallen debris. Central Texas takes hail and high winds annually, and most insurance policies will pay for a full replacement when shingles are bruised, cracked, or missing. We document the damage, work directly with your adjuster, and handle the claim.

How long does a roof replacement take?

Most shingle replacements on a single-story Austin home take 1 to 2 days. Standing seam metal roofs take 3 to 5 days. Larger or multi-roof-plane homes can take 4 to 7 days. Permits in Austin add 1 to 3 weeks before the crew shows up.

What's the lifespan of a roof in Austin's climate?

Architectural shingles in Austin's sun and storm climate typically last 18 to 25 years. Standing seam metal lasts 40 to 60 years. Synthetic slate and high-end shingles last 30 to 40. UV exposure and hail are the two factors that age Austin roofs fastest.
