Older houses, bigger lots, real trees

Manchaca is not a new subdivision and that changes the work. Lots are larger, the oaks are mature, and a lot of the housing stock predates the current round of South Austin building by decades.

Practically, three things follow. Repair is a bigger share of what we do here than anywhere else we work, because there is something already standing to repair. Tree work matters, because the best spot for a deck usually has an oak in it and the answer is building around the root plate rather than through it. And matching matters, because an addition on a 1970s house that reads as an addition costs you at resale.

Shade changes what is worth building

In most of the Austin area we are solving for too much sun. In Manchaca, under a mature canopy, that is frequently not the problem.

Which means an open cedar pergola is often enough here, where on a bare Kyle or Buda lot it would not be. The trees are already doing half the work. That is a cheaper build and we will tell you so rather than selling you a solid roof you do not need.

What mature shade does cause is moisture. Decks under heavy canopy stay damp longer, collect leaf litter in the gaps, and grow algae on the north side. Airflow underneath matters more here than anywhere, board spacing matters, and a deck that cannot dry rots from below. We build for that.

Permits out here

Most of Manchaca is unincorporated Travis County rather than inside the City of Austin, so permitting goes through the county and the rules are not identical. Some properties sit in Austin's extraterritorial jurisdiction, which is a third set of rules again. We establish which applies to your address before quoting, because it changes both the timeline and what is required.

Plenty of properties out here are on septic. If yours is, we locate the drain field before setting any footing. A pier through a lateral line is an expensive mistake and not a repairable one.

Manchaca questions, answered

Do you serve Manchaca and far south Austin?

Yes, regularly. Manchaca, Bear Creek, Shady Hollow, Circle C and the rest of far south Austin and northern Hays County. We are family owned, licensed and insured, and rated 5.0 on Google. John walks every estimate himself.

Do I need a City of Austin permit in Manchaca?

Often not, because most of Manchaca is unincorporated Travis County rather than inside the city. Permitting goes through the county instead, and some properties sit in Austin's extraterritorial jurisdiction, which is a third set of rules. We establish which applies to your address before quoting, since it changes both the timeline and what is required.

Is a pergola enough under mature trees?

Frequently, yes, and Manchaca is one of the few places we say that. Under a mature oak canopy the trees are already doing half the shading work, so an open cedar pergola often gets you where you want to be for considerably less than a solid roof. If you want rain cover, that is a different question and you do need a roof.

What should I know about building a deck under heavy tree cover?

Moisture. A deck under mature canopy stays damp longer, collects leaf litter in the board gaps and grows algae on the north side. Airflow underneath matters more here than anywhere else we build, and so does board spacing. A deck that cannot dry out rots from below, where you will not see it coming. We frame for drainage and leave the gaps that let it breathe.

Can you build around a mature oak?

Yes, and it usually makes the project better. We shift the footing layout or cantilever the framing so the structure reaches past the tree without putting a pier through its root zone. Cutting a generous opening and letting the trunk come up through a deck is often the best-looking version of the whole thing. What we will not do is cut a major root to place a post.

Do you serve Manchaca and the surrounding south Austin area?

Yes. We work across Manchaca (the road and surrounding neighborhoods are now officially named Menchaca, but most locals still say Manchaca), Bauerle Ranch, Shady Hollow, Circle C Ranch, and the rest of south Austin and northern Hays County.

Manchaca or Menchaca, which is correct?

Both refer to the same area in south Austin. The historical and most-used spelling is Manchaca, pronounced 'man-shack.' The City of Austin officially renamed the road to Menchaca in 2018, but the area, businesses, and locals still widely use Manchaca. We answer to either.

Do you handle Manchaca area HOA submissions?

Yes. South Austin neighborhoods like Bauerle Ranch, Shady Hollow, and Circle C have active HOAs with their own architectural review processes. We prepare and submit the full ARC packet for fences, decks, pergolas, and exterior changes as part of every project.
