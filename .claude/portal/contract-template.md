## 4. Schedule, delays and changes

- **Dates:** Work is expected to start on **[START DATE]** and finish by **[FINISH DATE]**. These are good-faith estimates, not guarantees.
- **Cancellation period first:** If section 10 applies, Work won't start until the cancellation period is over, unless Customer asks in writing to start sooner because of an emergency.
- **Delays:** The schedule extends, with no penalty to either side, for delays Contractor can't control. That includes weather, material shortages or late deliveries, permit, inspection or HOA timing, hidden conditions (section 6), Customer changes, and late payments.
- **Change orders:** Any change to the Work, price or schedule must be in a written change order signed by both parties, in the client portal or on paper (form in section 11). Contractor doesn't have to do extra work without a signed change order. Change order amounts are due with the next scheduled payment unless the change order says otherwise.
- **Verbal requests:** Requests made in person or by text are not approved changes until a change order is signed.
- **Customer delays:** If the job can't proceed because of Customer, such as no access, an unready site, HOA approval or late decisions, Contractor may move the job to its next open date.
- **Completion and punch list:** The Work is complete when it can be used for its intended purpose, even if small punch-list items remain. Customer and Contractor will do a final walkthrough and list any open items in writing. Final payment is due within 7 days after completion, and Customer may hold back only the reasonable cost of the listed items until they're finished.

## 6. Hidden conditions, materials and cleanup

- **Hidden conditions:** If Contractor finds something that couldn't be seen when the price was set, like rotted wood, termite damage, rotted roof decking, buried rock or debris, or existing work that isn't up to code, Contractor will stop that part of the Work, show Customer, and price the fix in a change order before going ahead. [ROOF JOBS ONLY: For roof jobs, replacing damaged decking costs [$ PER SHEET] per sheet.]
- **Natural materials:** Wood varies in color and grain, and it shrinks, swells, checks, cups and weathers over time. Cedar turns gray if it isn't stained. Concrete develops small cracks and color variation as it cures. These are normal and are not defects.
- **Substitutions:** If a selected material isn't available, Contractor may substitute one of equal or better quality with Customer's approval.
- **Cleanup:** Contractor will clean up the work area daily and haul off construction debris when the Work is done. Roof jobs include a magnetic nail sweep. Contractor will take reasonable care of the property, but some wear to grass along the access path from equipment and materials is normal.
- **Photos:** Contractor may photograph the finished Work for its website and marketing, without showing Customer's name or address.
- **Customer-supplied materials:** Contractor does not warranty materials Customer supplies, and isn't responsible for delays they cause.
- **Existing structures:** When the Work attaches to or relies on something already there, like the house, an existing roof, a slab or old posts, Contractor isn't responsible for the condition of that existing structure or for problems it causes.

### D. Disclosure statement (separate document, shown to Customer before they sign; Customer acknowledges receipt)

**Texas law requires your contractor to give you this brief overview of your rights, responsibilities and risks before you sign a residential construction contract.**

1. Your contractor can't require you to transfer your property to them as a condition of the contract.
2. Ask the contractor for references from past customers and check them.
3. Get a written contract that describes the work, when it will be done, the price or how the price is figured, and how and when payments are made, including retainage and final payment. Promises made only out loud may not be enforceable.
4. Read and understand every document before you sign it. You can have your own attorney review it. If a loan is involved, you're entitled to the closing documents at least one business day before closing unless you waive that for good cause.
5. Unless you sign a waiver, your contractor must give you a list of subcontractors and suppliers before construction starts, and update it when anyone new is added.
6. Check the work yourself or hire an inspector. Inspections by lenders or government offices are not quality control. Review any disbursement statements you receive.
7. If a subcontractor or supplier isn't paid, you may be responsible for paying them. If you get a written notice of claim, withhold that amount from your payments until it's resolved. During construction, and for 30 days after the work is completed, ended or abandoned, you should hold back 10 percent of payments.
8. Not every claim is valid. If you receive a notice of claim, consider talking to an attorney.
9. Before releasing money you held back, get signed and notarized releases from anyone who sent you a notice of claim. At final completion, your contractor must give you a bills-paid affidavit.
10. You may buy title insurance, and you can ask for a "completion of improvements" endorsement as a condition of making the final payment.
